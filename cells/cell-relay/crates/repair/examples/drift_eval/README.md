## What changed, and why the old comparison was unfair

Revision 1 reconstructed only the target in the naive arm, guaranteeing context
loss. It credited target recovery even when the whole write was wrong and mixed
refusals with safety without showing them. `baseline.v1.json` retains that run
only as historical evidence. Its 9/42 selective recovery and zero violations
are **not quality evidence** and are not numerically compared with revision 2.
The report version, baseline filename and trusted CI cache namespace all changed.

## Independent case labels

42 deterministic synthetic cases cover all seven §4a classes, six per class:

| Label | Per class | Total | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repairable | 2 | 14 | Migration evidence uniquely specifies a correct write; independent of current native capabilities |
| Ambiguous | 2 | 14 | Available evidence does not establish a unique mapping |
| Unsupported | 2 | 14 | Evidence needed to form a proposal is missing; no proposal is supplied |

Every label has a fixture rationale. Four null/type conversion cases remain in
the repairable denominator even though the current engine deliberately refuses
those operations. Labeling them unsupported because the engine refuses would
inflate the capability score.

Ambiguous cases have one candidate that is correct but below the repair gate,
and one incorrect candidate with deliberately overstated confidence (0.995).
The latter are **confidence-quality fault probes**, not measured detector error
rates. Their number and equal weighting are synthetic, not estimates of traffic
prevalence. Unsupported cases lack a usable mapping/source and supply no candidate.
There are no already-correct inputs to inflate recovery.

## Arms and oracle separation

All adapters accept only `Task` inputs and a drift class. The oracle's expected
write, labels, rationales and vendor acceptance rules are structurally separate
and inaccessible to adapter functions.

- No repair forwards the input.
- Naive full-payload reconstruction copies the **entire object**, preserves
Unrelated fields and nested context, and applies the supplied replacement or
Rename. It ignores confidence/identity gates. With no candidate it forwards the
Input. It neither reads the gold answer nor fabricates missing values.
- Selective runs native `agw-repair::repair` and `agw-policy::decide` in repair/write
Mode. Its actual assessments, failure and policy decision are retained.

This compares **candidate application and policy at explicit library seams**.
It does not generate candidates, run detectors, or exercise the full guard's
independent verifier. The policy seam receives `Verified` when the patcher
reports no unresolved findings; that is not proof of business intent. Wrong
supplied high-confidence candidates test precisely that trust boundary. This
is not evidence that the full guard or a default SDK would dispatch those writes.
Default `NullEngine`, production thresholds and runtime behavior are unchanged.

## Scoring

The fixture declares vendor required keys, accepted JSON types and optional enum
constraints separately from desired values. The vendor can reject malformed
shape while accepting a semantically wrong value. Null-meaning cases explicitly
allow null/number/string so that wrong meaning cannot be hidden as type rejection.

Every case/arm gets exactly one outcome:

- **Safe write:** vendor accepts the complete intended object, including preserved
Caller context. This alone earns recovery.
- **Unsafe write:** vendor accepts, but the full object violates intended values
Or changes unrelated context. This is a policy violation.
- **Blocked:** the policy seam does not dispatch. Neither recovery nor violation.
- **Rejected:** dispatch occurs, but the simulated vendor rejects the shape/enum.
  Neither recovery nor violation.

Successful repairs are safe writes that changed the input. Target-only success
is retained as a diagnostic and never earns recovery for an unsafe whole write.

Reports include overall recovery (`safe writes / all cases`), repairable recovery
(`safe writes in independently repairable cases / repairable cases`), repairable
misses, successful repairs, blocks, rejections and violations. Violation rates
are reported both per all cases and per dispatched cases. No dispatches means
**N/A**, never zero risk. Class and label slices are mandatory. JSON counts are
exact; derived floating rates permit only JSON round-trip rounding differences
(1e-15) during validation. Regression decisions use exact per-case outcomes.

## Observed revision 2 results after

Each arm evaluates the same 42 cases; the repairable subset contains 14.

| Arm | Safe recovery overall | Recovery on repairable cases | Successful repairs | Blocks | Upstream rejections | Unsafe writes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No repair | 0/42 | 0/14 | 0 | 0 | 12 | 30 |
| Naive full-payload reconstruction | 21/42 | 14/14 | 21 | 0 | 5 | 16 |
| Native selective repair + policy (after ) | 11/42 | 11/14 | 11 | 21 | 6 | 4 |

Unsafe writes per dispatch are 30/42, 16/42 and **4/21**, respectively. The native
arm's lower dispatch volume is visible rather than hidden inside zero violations.
Its three repairable misses are required numeric type-transition refusals. Its four unsafe
writes are the deliberately overconfident wrong unit, timestamp, enum and date
proposals. Both repair arms have zero violations on the repairable slice.

Adds only the explicit, source-validated empty-string-to-null repair. The
committed original v2 baseline retains 10/14 recovery; the new run passes against
it with 11/14. Inputs, independent labels, facts, thresholds and scoring remain
unchanged. The three numeric misses remain in the denominator. No baseline was
rewritten to obtain the improvement.

This identifies a capability limit and an input-confidence trust limit; it does
not establish real-world recovery, an optimal threshold, or an overall winner.
The naive arm's 21 safe writes include seven coincidentally correct ambiguous
proposals; that does not make those proposals justified by available evidence.

## Regression and CI

CI covers all cell/prototype changes, contracts and Rust configuration. A run
compares with the checked-in v2 baseline, independently fetched PR-base v2 report,
and last successful trusted main v2 run. Only successful main pushes promote the
trusted cache. Missing caches fall back explicitly to committed evidence; a v2
introduction with no base v2 report is reported explicitly. V1 is never reused.

Missing metrics, incompatible corpus bytes/version, changed IDs/labels, changed
counts or inconsistent derived rates fail validation. Each case in every arm
must retain recovery and may not acquire an unsafe write. Selective violations
also cannot exceed no-repair in a class/label slice. Always blocking fails on
lost recovery; target-only repairs cannot conceal unsafe writes. Intentional
safety/recovery tradeoffs require review, not an aggregate score that silently
chooses the winner.

After a genuine improvement, review `target/drift-eval/current.json` before
updating `baseline.v2.json`. PR-base and trusted-run comparisons still apply.
Corpus or scoring changes require an explicit version migration rather than
quietly overwriting a comparable baseline. No thresholds are selected automatically.
